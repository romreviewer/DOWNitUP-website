Detailed Comparison

A closer look at how DOWNitUP and Internet Download Manager stack up.

Price is the most obvious difference. IDM is shareware: you get a 30-day trial, and after that you need to buy a license, which runs around $10 to $25 depending on the license type and where you buy it. Prices change, so check their site for the current figure, but the model stays the same. DOWNitUP is simply free: every feature, every update, no trial countdown and no license key to keep track of.

Platform support is the second big gap. IDM is a Windows-only application, and has been for its entire history. If you switch between a Windows PC at work, a Mac at home, and an Android phone in your pocket, IDM only covers one of those. DOWNitUP runs natively on Windows, macOS, Linux, and Android, so the same download queue and habits follow you across devices. We cover this in more detail in our comparison of IDM, FDM, and DOWNitUP.

Finally, there's torrent support. IDM handles HTTP and HTTPS downloads well, but it can't open a magnet link. DOWNitUP combines multi-connection HTTP acceleration with a built-in BitTorrent client, so regular files and torrents live in one app instead of two. Both managers resume interrupted downloads, so you won't lose progress either way.

When IDM Is Still the Better Choice

To be fair, IDM has earned its reputation. Its browser integration on Windows is extremely deep: it can sniff out streaming videos on a page and offer a one-click grab, and its plugin slots into Chrome, Edge, and Firefox almost invisibly. If downloading embedded video from websites is your main use case, IDM does that job very well.

It also makes sense to stick with IDM if you only ever use Windows and you already own a license. There's no reason to abandon a tool you've paid for and configured to your liking just because a free option exists. IDM is a mature, stable product, and for a Windows-only workflow it remains a solid pick.

When DOWNitUP Is the Better Choice

DOWNitUP pulls ahead the moment you step outside Windows. If you work across macOS, Linux, and Android, or you simply want the same download manager on your phone and your desktop, IDM can't follow you there. DOWNitUP can. And if you ever download torrents, having BitTorrent built into the same app as your HTTP downloads saves you from juggling two tools.

Then there's the price. Paying for a download manager made sense a decade ago; today a free, modern, ad-free app with the same multi-connection acceleration is hard to argue with.
